> **(ii)** A **domain qualification** is added to `[A,D] = −I`: it holds on a *common core*, and

The native scaling parameter and the partition displacement coordinate describe **different
operations**, and no useful bridge survives. The partition dictionary is exact; its hyperbolic and
radial coordinates are exact reparameterisations that **do not** expose any operator identity,
generator relation, or positivity structure invisible in `δ`. One of them, `η`, is strictly worse
than `δ` by two measurable criteria.

This is the brief's payoff **E (clean no-go)**, proved rather than merely unfound.

## FINAL QUESTION

> Does `η = arctanh(2δ)` merely reparameterize off-line displacement, or is it naturally generated
> by the same intrinsic scaling structure that appears in the Sonin/Weil framework?

**It merely reparameterizes.** It is an exact coordinate, but not the natural group parameter — and
*(corrected 2026-10-07)* it does not "break" anything: the generator identity is a fact about `δ`
and survives any relabelling. What fails is that the relabelled family has no constant generator.

The off-line displacement *is* exactly generated by a one-parameter semigroup, and the parameter is
**`δ` itself**:

```
F(gamma + i delta) = \widehat{ e^{-delta A} f }(gamma),     A = multiplication by u on L^2[-L,L],
```

an identity at all orders, verified to `≤ 3.7e-17` for three test functions at
`δ = 0.05, 0.2, 0.375`. The native composition is therefore `e^{−δ₁A}e^{−δ₂A} = e^{−(δ₁+δ₂)A}` —
**plain addition in `δ`** (deviation `≤ 3.8e-17`, against `2.1e-04`–`4.5e-03` for the Möbius
composite). Since `2δ = tanh η`, additivity in `η` would require Möbius addition
`(δ₁+δ₂)/(1+4δ₁δ₂)`, which the problem does not have. **`η` is additive for the wrong law.**

Precisely: writing the same family in rapidity, `V_η := e^{−½tanh(η)A}`, gives
`dV_η/dη = −½ sech²(η)·A·V_η` — an **`η`-dependent** generator (verified to `1.7e-11`). So
`V_{η₁}V_{η₂} = V_{η₁+η₂}` iff `tanh η₁ + tanh η₂ = tanh(η₁+η₂)`, which fails by `2.66e-02` at
`(0.2,0.3)`, `1.63e-01` at `(0.5,0.5)` and `5.40e-01` at the quasi-RH endpoint
`(0.9729551, 0.9729551)`. **You can relabel the displacement using rapidity; that does not make the
operation obey rapidity addition.**

## The positive outcome — what the comparison actually bought

> **Off-line displacement is exponential reweighting of the window, not the Sonin scaling action.**
> The window does not move; the contribution from different positions inside it is reweighted —
> for `δ > 0` one side amplified, the other attenuated, every position fixed.

This is the classical transform relationship between spectral translation and exponential
weighting, not a new arithmetic mechanism. It also explains the second moment with no reference to
rapidity: expanding `e^{−δu} = 1 − δu + δ²u²/2 − ⋯`, the count-preserving quartet comparison
cancels the odd powers of `δ`, and what survives at second order is built from the **first and
second coordinate moments** of the window — which is what `F'(γ)` and `F''(γ)` are. So

> **coordinate moments enter the sensitivity law because exponential reweighting differentiates
> into powers of the coordinate.**

That is simpler and better founded than the proposed bridge, and it is the thing to keep. It does
**not** make the response a nonnegative norm: `K(L,γ)` controls the derivative-evaluation piece,
while `F'² + FF''` carries either sign — which Theorem 8 and Remark 10 already distinguish.

## The single strongest mathematical statement established

> **Two no-gos, both proved.**
>
> **(1) Composition.** The only native composition law on off-line displacement is the semigroup
> law of `e^{−δA}`, which is **additive in `δ`**. The rapidity coordinate's additivity corresponds
> to Möbius addition, which is not it. Moreover additive composition **leaves** the partition
> domain (`0.375 + 0.375 = 0.75 > ½`) while Möbius composition **cannot** (`0.4800 < ½`) — a
> "broken domain" in the Two-Face manuscript's own §6.6 sense, which its §6.4 classifies as a
> **true mismatch**.
>
> **(2) Generators.** The intrinsic scaling generator `D = d/du` (from
> `ϑ(λ)ξ(v) = λ^{-1/2}ξ(λ^{-1}v)`, translation in `u = log v`, skew-adjoint, unitary group) and the
> displacement generator `A = u·` (multiplication, self-adjoint, non-unitary semigroup) satisfy
>
>   **`[A, D] = −I`**  — exact over `Q`, verified on `p = 1`, `p = u` and an arbitrary quartic,
>   **on a common core** (polynomials on `(−L,L)`, or `C_c^∞(−L,L)`).
>
> *Domain qualification, added 2026-10-07:* this is **not** a claim about two globally defined
> generators on one fixed-window space. `e^{tD}` is **not** a unitary group on `L²[−L,L]` —
> translation moves support out, retaining at most `0.938, 0.750, 0.500, 0.000` of the mass at
> `t = 0.1, 0.4, 0.8, 1.6` for `L = 0.8`, so zero-extended window functions are not a
> translation-invariant space — and `D` with no boundary condition is **not** skew-adjoint there:
> for `f = 1+u`, `g = 2−u` the defect `⟨Df,g⟩+⟨f,Dg⟩ = 1.6000000` **equals the boundary term
> exactly**. `A` is bounded self-adjoint on `L²[−L,L]` (`‖A‖ = L`); `D` generates translations on
> `L²(R)`, where the scaling action lives. **And a relationship between two operators is not an
> identification of them**: `[A,D] = −I` supplies neither the missing semi-local Sonin–Weil trace
> comparison nor any positivity bound.
>
> They are a **Heisenberg pair**: non-commuting and exactly dual under Fourier/Mellin. Off-line
> displacement is an *imaginary* shift of the spectral ordinate; Sonin scaling is a *real* shift of
> the physical coordinate. So `t = η` is unjustified — **and so is `t = δ`.** Duality is not
> identity.

Of the brief's §4 outcomes: **outcome 1** (exact generator correspondence) holds for `δ`;
**outcome 4** (incompatibility) holds for `η`, because matching `e^{ηA}+e^{−ηA}` would require
`A² = u²tanh²η/(4η²)`, which is `η`-dependent and therefore not an operator. **Outcome 3 (formal
Taylor similarity) is not claimed as a bridge anywhere.**

## Why `η` and `R` are not improvements — the measurable part

| test | `δ` / `χ` | `η` | `R` |
|---|---|---|---|
| remainder `B_L(δ)`, rewritten **exactly** | baseline | **identical** (dev `0`) | **identical** (dev `0`) |
| remainder, **truncated** at leading order (this budget, `L=0.8`, `\|δ\|≤3/8` — **not** a general claim about coordinate changes) | — | `1.22×` worse at `δ=0.25`, `1.89×` at `δ=3/8` | `1.36×` worse, `2.88×` |

| monotonicity | all four coordinates are strictly monotone in each other, so monotonicity statements are **logically equivalent** — nothing can be gained | | |
| convexity | linear in `δ²` | convex then **concave**, inflection at `δ = 0.2886751`, **inside** `\|δ\| ≤ 3/8` | **concave**, `d²/dR² = −1/(2R³)` |

The convexity row is the point: convexity is the one property a nonlinear monotone change does not
preserve, and it is therefore **coordinate-dependent** — not a statement about the Weil form. Any
convexity "theorem" in `R` or `η` would be an artifact.

**An error avoided, and named.** `δ = ½√(1−1/R)` has a branch point at `R = 1` — the RH point —
and it is tempting to report that as structure. It is not: `Φ` is **even** in `δ`, so it depends on
`R` only through the rational `(R−1)/(4R)` and is analytic at `R = 1`. No branch point is claimed.

## What is NOT claimed

Not claimed, anywhere: that RH is a binary-partition physical system; that Fisher, Poincaré or
Compound metrics govern zeta zeros; that hyperbolic geometry proves RH; that the manuscript's
equipartition-differential empirical results transfer to zero distributions; or that special
relativity and RH share dynamics. The exact available statement is only that `(½+δ, ½−δ)` is a
conserved binary partition of the real parts of a reflected pair.

**No numerical conditioning claim is made either**: the coordinate changes were never evaluated for
conditioning, and improved conditioning after a coordinate change would not be new arithmetic
structure.
