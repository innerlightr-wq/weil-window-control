== 1. the bound, and the toy counterexample which must satisfy it ==
   P_0 = (z^2+1)(z^2+4):  Delta = 2;  the competitor of z = i is z = 2i,
   at ordinate separation R = |Re(i) - Re(2i)| = 0.
  PASS  R = 0 < Delta/sqrt2 = 1.4142136 
   NOTE: the minimal counterexample is CONFLUENT IN THE ORDINATE (R = 0).  That is
   the same configuration the repository's
   extensions/moderate-gap-spectral-bridge-2026-10-21/06_CONFLUENT_BLIND_SPOTS.md
   identifies as structural: an off-line zero's functional-equation partner always
   sits at ordinate distance exactly 0.  Here the partner supplies the -1, and a
   SECOND, DEEPER orbit at the same ordinate is what overturns it.

== 3. the bound in zeta's own units ==
   zeta's trivial strip gives delta_max <= 1/2, so Delta <= 1 and the single-
   competitor condition is  |gamma_j - gamma_k| < 1/(2 sqrt 2) = 0.3535533906.
   The mean zeta zero spacing is 2 pi/log(gamma/2 pi):
     gamma =   100:  mean spacing 2.2705167   -> above the threshold
     gamma =   1e6:  mean spacing 0.52457652   -> above the threshold
     gamma =  1e12:  mean spacing 0.24359905   -> BELOW the threshold
     gamma =  1e30:  mean spacing 0.09344461   -> BELOW the threshold
   The mean spacing equals the threshold 1/(2 sqrt2) at
     gamma* = 2 pi exp(4 pi sqrt 2) = 328290294.5  (about 3.3 x 10^8),
   so the condition is vacuous at low height and becomes satisfiable by TYPICAL
   neighbouring ordinates only above gamma*  -- and by Lehmer-type pairs much
   earlier (the pair 6709/6710 at gamma = 7005.06 has |d gamma| = 0.0377, well
   inside the threshold 0.3536 already).  So the condition does NOT rule out
   non-monotonicity for zeta; it localises where it could occur.  NO CLAIM IS MADE
   THAT IT DOES OCCUR -- that would be a statement about off-line zeros of zeta.

== 4. what the bound is and is not ==
   * NECESSARY, not sufficient: it says where a counterexample must live.
   * It uses only the sign identity and the strip half-width -- no arithmetic, so
     it does NOT distinguish zeta from Davenport-Heilbronn.
   * It gives no information at tau = 0 beyond what the strip width already gives.
   * It is the one statement here that is both new and cheap to apply.
