DESCRIPTION

Let Q be the Weil explicit-formula quadratic form restricted to test functions
supported in a compact window [-L, L], in one fixed normalization, and let
lambda_Q(L) be its spectral infimum. This technical note reports
computer-assisted two-sided bounds at two explicit windows:

    1.43300048827856e-17 <= lambda_Q(4/5) <= 1.674e-17
    8.27343868254787e-18 <= lambda_Q(L~)  <= 9.475e-18

where L~ = 80471895621705018/10^17 is a rational strictly below (log 5)/2.

Each lower bound comes from a frequency-cutoff comparison at T = 400, reduced to
a 440-dimensional Legendre block whose positive definiteness is verified by a
preconditioned congruence in Arb ball arithmetic, together with a
super-exponentially small complement estimate and a strict Schur-type coupling
test; both parity sectors are certified at each window. Each upper bound is the
Rayleigh quotient of the original form at one explicit even rational test
function, evaluated in closed form through the digamma, Lerch Phi and Hurwitz
zeta functions, a route that uses no quadrature at all and therefore has no
truncation term to bound.

Because the form restricts consistently to smaller windows, lambda_Q is
non-increasing in L. The first lower bound therefore holds throughout
0 < L <= 4/5, the second throughout 0 < L <= L~, and the second upper bound for
every L >= L~, including exactly (log 5)/2. No positive lower bound at
(log 5)/2 follows from these certificates. Pairing the four endpoints gives a
corollary on the true minima, 0.494 <= lambda_Q(L~)/lambda_Q(4/5) <= 0.662, so
the spectral infimum falls by between 33.8 and 50.6 percent between the two
windows.

At L = 4/5 the note also certifies, per parity sector, that removing four
explicit rational directions from the even sector raises the infimum to at least
0.6175 (five directions: 0.8534; three odd directions: 0.1068; four odd:
0.7841), and that exactly four even eigenvalues of Q lie below 1e-2 — at most
four from the conditioning certificate, at least four from a certified
four-dimensional test space for Q itself. In the odd sector only "at most three"
is certified. A proved normalization dictionary identifies this form with the
window form of Andrews, Connes and La Gatta (arXiv:2611.13129v1) under
lambda = exp(L), the two archimedean normalizations differing by exactly
2 log 2; the identity is corroborated numerically to about thirty digits by an
independent implementation.

All constants are published as midpoint-radius enclosures with the exact
rational input vectors and the code that produced them. A release gate shipped
with the package replays the whole chain from the packaged files (61 checks) and
exits nonzero on any failure. An appendix records the errors found during the
work, several of which produced plausible wrong numbers or unreplayable exports
rather than outright failures.

SCOPE. This is a computation at two windows. It establishes no arithmetic
estimate, no statement about the location of any zero of the Riemann zeta
function, no verified range of zeros, no result at any window above L~, and
nothing about the Riemann hypothesis. No open question of arXiv:2611.13129v1 is
settled here, and the conditioning spaces used are not identified with the
prolate spaces of that paper. The finite-reduction strategy is taken from
arXiv:2608.24827v2 and credited there; the parity decoupling is likewise not
new, and that paper's Theorem 6.2 establishes the simplicity and evenness of the
ground state at L = 4/5, which this note does not. The computation inherits its
zeta-window normalization, geometric-side assembly, pole conventions and
closed-form evaluator from the author's earlier sensitivity note
(doi:10.5281/zenodo.23250932), and its even/odd pole signs were checked
entrywise against that assembly in the author's real-zero parity note
(doi:10.5281/zenodo.23624161); neither is an input to the lower bounds proved
here. Technical note, not peer-reviewed. No priority is claimed.
